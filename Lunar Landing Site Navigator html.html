Landing readiness ·

    Path to touchdown: how a mission clears every challenge
    1. Pick the region: long illumination, ice access, gentle slopes (Shackleton rim, Malapert, Nobile, de Gerlache).
    2. Pick the date window: use the timeline to find stretches with Sun and Earth both up. Launch dates follow from this.
    3. Transfer and orbit: arrive when lighting and relay geometry match the landing window; plan a relay or orbiter for comms when Earth is low.
    4. Precision descent: hazard-relative navigation to land within ~100 m on slopes under ~10°.
    5. Commission fast: use the first joint Sun + Earth days for deployment, checkout and uplink.
    6. Survive the night: size batteries or heaters for the longest dark gap, or move to a better-lit rim.
    Model: simplified analytic Sun/Earth ephemeris in the Moon-fixed frame (phase, libration, solar declination), roughly ±1–2° and a few hours. Site coordinates are approximate; add your own. Horizon mask is a uniform terrain angle. Use SPICE or JPL Horizons for flight-grade work.
